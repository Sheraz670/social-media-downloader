What if the only thing standing between you and your dream was a single unpaid internship, a tiny apartment that turns into a cardboard box, and a child who believes you can still make it? That’s the premise of “The Pursuit of Happyness,” a 2006 biographical drama that follows the real-life story of Chris Gardner, a struggling salesman who refuses to give up on his future and his son.

The film opens in the early 1990s in San Francisco, where Chris Gardner (played by Will Smith) is a charismatic, yet under‑employed salesman. He’s trying to sell expensive medical equipment to doctors, but the market is tough. Despite his relentless optimism, he can’t seem to land a stable job. He’s living with his girlfriend, Linda, and their young son, Christopher Jr., and the financial pressure is mounting. The first real test comes when Chris learns that he needs to pay a substantial amount of money for a new apartment and the cost of his son’s future. He decides to leave Linda and take his son with him, hoping that a fresh start in a new city will bring better opportunities.

Chris then moves to a cramped apartment in a downtown building, where he shares a small space with a neighbor who is also struggling. He is forced to juggle the demands of his job search with the needs of his son. He tries to keep his son’s life normal by taking him to the park and to a baseball game, but the reality of their situation is hard to ignore. He has to fight for a place to sleep and find a way to keep his son from falling into the same cycle of poverty that he fears he might one day fall into.

The turning point comes when Chris learns about a prestigious, unpaid internship program at a brokerage firm, Dean Witter. He sees it as a chance to break out of the cycle, so he applies, hoping it will lead to a permanent position. The internship is grueling, but Chris works hard, earning the respect of his colleagues and the mentorship of a senior partner, who sees potential in him. He learns the ins and outs of the stock market, mastering the art of the pitch, and begins to see a future that was previously unimaginable.

But the road to success is not linear. While Chris is working, he is still on the brink of homelessness. He and his son are forced to sleep in a hotel’s basement, then a subway tunnel, and eventually a cardboard box in a park. He tries to keep his son safe and out of trouble, all while juggling a demanding internship and the looming threat of eviction. The film shows the raw reality of homelessness: the fear, the uncertainty, and the constant battle to keep a sense of hope alive.

The climax arrives when Chris finally secures a full-time position at the brokerage firm. He is offered a role as a stockbroker, a job that will pay well and provide stability for his family. The final scenes show him walking into a new office, with a new apartment, and a future that is bright and full of possibility. He finally realizes that his pursuit of happiness has not just been about financial success, but about the love for his son and his own resilience.

In the end, the film is not just a story about a man who overcomes adversity, but a testament to the power of perseverance. Chris Gardner’s story reminds us that the pursuit of happiness is often a long, winding road, full of obstacles, but that with determination, hope, and the willingness to keep pushing forward, it’s possible to turn those obstacles into stepping stones toward a brighter future.
